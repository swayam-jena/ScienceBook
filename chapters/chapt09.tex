\section{I Explore}
Earth appears to be round when viewed form space, but its shape is closer to that of an oblate sphariod. It flattens at the poles and buldges out at the Equator. It has no corners, no beginning and no end. So, it is very difficult to locate places on Earth. Therefore, we use some imaginary fixed points and lines on the earth's surface to help us locate different places on it.
\vspace{0.1in}
\par
The North Pole, the South Pole and the Equator are such fixed points and lines.
\vspace{0.1in}
\par
Let us read about these imaginary points and lines in detail.
\section{\textcolor{red}{Parallels or Lines of Latitude}}
The imaginary \textcolor{red}{horizontal} lines that run from east to west are called \textbf{lines of latitude.} Earth is divided into two equal halves, the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere, by an imaginary line. This line is called the \textbf{Equator.} The lines of latitude run at an equal distance from each other and do not meet at all. So, they are also called the \textcolor{red}{parallels} of latitude. The Equator is the longest latitude. All latitudes are complete circles, except for the North Pole and the South Pole. These are points. The length of the latitude keeps getting shorter as we move away from the Equator and towards the poles.
\subsection{\textcolor{orange}{How are Latitudes Numbered?}}
The latitudes are measured in degrees. They are given numbers starting at 0 degree, which is the Equator. We mark the other latitudes from 0 degree to 90 degree. The latitudes in the Northern Hemisphere are denoted by the letter 'N', while the latitudes in the Southern Hemisphere are denoted by letter 'S'. The North Pole is written as 90 degree N. The South Pole is written as 90 degree S. latitudes are drawn at intervals of one degree. There are 90 parallels in the Northern Hemisphere and 90 in the Southern Hemisphere. Thus, there are 181 parallels in all including the Equator.
\subsection{\textcolor{Orange}{Important Lines of Latitude}}
The seven important lines of latitude are as follows.
\vspace{0.1in}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Equator (0 degree)
    \item Tropic of Cancer (23.5 degree N)
    \item Tropic of Capricorn (23.5 degree S)
    \item Arctic Circle (66.5 degree N)
    \item Antarctic Circle (66.5 degree S)
    \item North Pole (90 degree N)
    \item South Pole (90 degree S)
\end{itemize}
\section{\textcolor{red}{Meridians or Lines of Longitudes}}
\textbf{Lines of Longitude} or meridians are imaginary lines that run vertically from north to south. They are not parallel. Unlike the parallels of latitude, all meridians are of equal length. Meridians are semicircles drawn from one pole to the other.
\vspace{0.1in}
\par
Meridians cut the parallels at the right angles (90 degree). The meridians are also measured in degrees. The distance between two meridians is the widest near the Equator, where Earth bulges out. All meridians come closer at the North and South poles and the distance is reduced to zero.
\vspace{0.5in}
\par
Like the Equator, the Prime Meridian also divides the globes into two hemispheres, the Eastern Hemisphere and the Western Hemisphere. The Eastern Hemisphere is towards the right and the Western Hemisphere is towards the left of the Prime Meridian. The Prime Meridian is the line of 0 degree longitude.
\vspace{0.1in}
\par
The degree of longitude is specified by the letter 'E' for east and the letter 'W' for west. They help to locate the exact position of a place.
\subsection{\textcolor{orange}{How are Lines of Longitude Numbered?}}
The lines of longitudes are drawn at intervals of one degree. There are a total of 360 longitudes-180 longitudes towards the east of the Prime Meridian and 180 longitudes towards the west of the prime meridian. The longitude of 180 degree E and the longitude of 180 degree W fall on the same line, which is exactly opposite to the Prime Meridian. Therefore, it is not labelled as 'E' or 'W'. So, in total, there are 360 lines of longitude and they are of the same length.
\vspace{0.1in}
\par
The 180th longitude is selected as the International Date Line because it separates two calendar days. The countries in the Eastern Hemisphere are one day ahead of the countries in the Eastern Hemisphere are one day ahead of the countries in Western Hemisphere. For example, if it is 15 March in the Eastern Hemisphere, it will be 14 March in the Western Hemisphere.
\vspace{0.32in}
\section{\textcolor{red}{Grid of Latitude and Longitude}}
The longitudes and latitudes from a network of interesting lines on maps and globes called the \textcolor{red}{grid}. The grid helps on maps and globes. To locate a place, we look at its latitude (degree N or degree S) and its longitude (degree E or degree W). The point where these two lines intersect or cut each other is the location of that place. For example, a place \textbf{P} is located at 40 degree N latitude and 60 degree W. Find out where these two lines cut each other. That point will be the location of the place P.

\section{Now I Know}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The imaginary lines that circle Earth's surface, running east and west parallel to the Equator are called parallels or lines of latitudes.
    \item The Equator is the longest latitude at 0 degree.
    \item The poles are at the farthest points form the Equator at i90 degree.
    \item The imaginary lines of equal lengths that run vertically on the globe in the north-south direction are called the meridians or lines of longitude.
    \item There are a total of 360 longitudes in the Eastern hemisphere and the Western Hemisphere.
    \item The lengths of all the lines of longitudes are equal.
    \item The Prime Meridian is the 0 degree longitude that divides Earth into two equal hemispheres, the Eastern Hemisphere and the Western Hemisphere.
    \item The 180th longitude is called the International Date Line.
    \item The International Date Line separates two calendar days. The countries in the Eastern Hemisphere are one day ahead of the countries in the Western Hemisphere.
    \item Latitudes and longitudes cut each other at right angles and form a network of lines on the globe. This is called the grid.
    \item This grid helps us locate places on maps and globes.
\end{itemize}
