\section{\textcolor{red}{What to Know}}
Earth's motion causes the formation of day and night as well as change of seasons.
\section{I Explore}
Earth is a spherical in shape. It is slightly bulged at the Equator and flattened at the poles. The Equator, an \textcolor{red}{imaginary} line, divides Earth into two equal parts.
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
The northern half of Earth is called the Northern Hemisphere and the southern half is called the Southern Hemisphere. The word 'hemisphere' was derived from the Greek word 'hemisphairion'. It means one half of a spherical body.
\par
Approximately 67.3 percent of Earth's landmasses and around 60.7 percent of its water bodies are situated in the Northern Hemisphere. It is estimated that around 87 percent of the world's population resides in the Northern Hemisphere. In contrast, the Southern Hemisphere has only about 32.7 percent of Earth's landmasses is located in the Southern Hemisphere.
\section{\textcolor{red}{Movements of Earth}}
In the ancient times, people believed that Earth is \textcolor{red}{stationary} and heavenly bodies move around it.
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
About 2300 years ago, Aristarchus of Samos, a Greek astronomer, found out that Earth rotates on its axis and goes around the Sun.
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
Later, in the 1500s Nicolaus Copernicus, a Polish astronomer, also found out about Earth's daily movement on its axis and its yearly movement around the Sun. Thus, there are two movements of Earth that happens at the same time.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Movement on its axis
    \vspace{0.1in}
    \item Movement around the Sun.
\end{enumerate}
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
To understand these movements, let us begin by understanding what an axis is.
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
Like all spinning objects, Earth also spins on its own axis. Earth's axis is imaginary line passing through its centre. The two end points of the axis are called the poles. There are two poles-the North Pole and the South Pole. The axis of Earth is tilted at an angle of 23.5 degree. Earth spins counterclockwise, that is, from west to east. That is why it seems like the Sun rises in the east and sets in the west.
\subsection{\textcolor{orange}{Rotation}}
The spinning movement of Earth on its own axis is called \textbf{rotation.} Earth takes 24 hours to complete one rotation. It is due to the \textcolor{red}{incline} of Earth's axis that the lengths of day and night are not to same throughout the year at different places on Earth.
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
An interesting way to understand how Earth rotates is to make a 'thumbs-up' sign with your right hand. Your thumb will point upwards and the rest of your fingers will curl. Your thumb is Earth's rotation axis, pointing to the North Pole and your fingers are curling in the direction of Earth's rotation
\subsection{\textbf{Formation of day and night}}
We experience day and night due to the rotation of Earth on its axis. Due to its spherical shape, only the half of Earth facing the Sun receives sunlight. This half with sunlight falling on it experiences day. But the other half that is turned away from the Sun does not receive any light and so it remains dark and experiences night. All parts of Earth alternately get day and night because of its continuous rotation.
\par
\vspace{0.23in}
Let us do an activity to understand the formation of day and night.
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
\textbf{Materials required:} A globe and a torch on a table lamp
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
\textbf{Method:}
\vspace{0.1in}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Place the globe on a table in a dark room. Place the lamp on the table at some distance from the globe and switch it on.
    \item You will see that the side of the globe facing the lamp and receives the light of the lamp and the other side that does not recieve the lamp's light is   dark. Note down the names of three countries each lying in the lit half and the dark half.
    \item Now, rotate the globe one full time slowly from the left to the right. You will note that the countries that were in the lit part have now moved into the darkness. The countries that were in the dark have now moved towards light.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{0.1in}
\par
\textbf{Conclusion:} Rotation of Earth causes the formation of day and night. Different places on Earth experiences day and night at different times.
\par
\vspace{0.17in}
\subsection{\textcolor{orange}{Revolution}}
Earth not only rotates on its axis but also moves around the Sun in a fixed oval-shaped path called its \textcolor{red}{orbit}. The movement of Earth around the Sun in its orbit is called \textbf{revolution.} Earth revolves in an anticlockwise or counterclockwise direction, that is, from west to east, just like the rotating movement. As Earth is revolving around the Sun, it is also rotating on its axis.
\par
\vspace{0.4in}
Earth takes 365 days and 6 hours to complete one revolution around the Sun. The remaining 6 hours are added up in 4 years to make 24 hours. This extra day is added to the month of February every four years. Usually, February has 28 days, but every fourth year, it has 29 days. The year in which there are 366 days instead of 365 days is called a \textbf{leap year.}

\subsection{\textbf{Change of seasons}}
The change of seasons on Earth happens due to two factors:
\vspace{0.1in}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tilt of Earth's axis
    \item Revolution of Earth around the Sun
\end{itemize}
\par
\vspace{0.1in}
To understand this, look at the diagram of the cycle of seasons below.
\vspace{0.54in}
Look at \textbf{Position 2.} The North Pole is tiled towards the Sun, and the sunrays fall directly on it. So, the places in the Northern Hemisphere receive more light and heat from the Sun. Therefore, it is summer in this hemisphere. The days are long and nights are short. During this time, places in the Southern Hemisphere are tilted away from the Sun. The Southern Hemisphere gets less sunlight. In is winter there. The nights are longer than the days.
\vspace{0.1in}
\par
Differet parts of Earth receive Sun's direct rays at different times of the year. Look at \textbf{Position 4}, the South Pole is tiled toward the Sun. Therefore, it is summer in this hemisphere. When the South Pole tilts toward the Sun, it is winter in the Northern Hemisphere. Since the tilt of the axis is 23.5 degrees, the sunrays never point directly at the North Pole.
\vspace{0.1in}
\par
The Northern Hemisphere begins to tilt away from the Sun. Look at \textbf{Position 3}. In this position, neither the Northern Hemisphere nor the Southern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun. The sunrays fall directly on the Equator. It is autumn in the Northern Hemisphere and Spring in Southern Hemisphere. It is neither very hot nor very cold in both the hemispheres.
\vspace{0.1in}
Look at \textbf{Position 1}. In this position, the Northern Hemisphere again begins to tilt towards the Sun and the weather starts getting warmer again. The sunrays fall on the Equator once again. It is spring in the Northern Hemisphere and autumn in the Southern Hemisphere.

\section{Now I Know}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Earth is a spherical but Slightly flat at the poles.
    \item The Equator divides Earth into two equal halves-the upper half, called the Northern Hemisphere and the lower half, called the Southern Hemisphere.
    \item The Sun appears to rise in the east and set in the west due to the rotation of Earth from west to east.
    \item An axis is an imaginary line that runs through the centre of spinning objects.
    \item Earth's axis is tilted at 23.5 degree.
    \item The movement of Earth on its own axis once in 24 hours is called rotation. The rotation of Earth causes day and night.
    \item The movement of Earth around the Sun in a fixed orbit is called a revolution. Earth takes 365 days and 6 hours to revolve once around the Sun.
    \item A leap year had 366 days. We have a leap year after every four years.
    \item The revolution of Earth causes the cycle of seasons.
    \item Earth rotates on its own axis and revolves around the Sun at the Same time.
\end{itemize}
